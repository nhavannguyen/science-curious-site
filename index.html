Why Lab Stories Matter

The sanitized version of science — hypothesis, method, result — hides the chaos. Real discoveries happen in messy notebooks, after failed experiments, during late-night conversations over cold pizza. These stories deserve to be told.

"In science, there is no such thing as a failed experiment — only experiments with unexpected results." — R. Buckminster Fuller

Behind every Nature paper are hundreds of hours of troubleshooting, reagent mix-ups, and existential crises in the tissue culture room. I write about those hours because they're where the real humanity of science lives.

Survival Tips for New Lab Members

  • → Label everything. Then label the label. Future-you will be grateful.
  • → Never trust a protocol you haven't read three times. At minimum.
  • → The -80°C freezer is sacred ground. Treat it with reverence.
  • → When in doubt, make a spreadsheet. When still in doubt, ask a postdoc.

Each piece begins with a scientific concept I find beautiful — a protein structure, a phase diagram, or a cellular process. I translate the data into visual language using 3D modeling, illustration, and design principles borrowed from fine art.

"Art is not what you see, but what you make others see." — Edgar Degas. Science art makes the invisible visible.

Commissions are open for scientific illustrations, conference posters, and educational infographics. Every project starts with a conversation about what makes your research beautiful.

Why Sci-Pop?

Science communication doesn't have to be boring. The best explanations meet people where they are — in their kitchens, on their feeds, in their daily curiosities. Every latte, every sunset, every sneeze is a science lesson waiting to happen.

I write Sci-Pop pieces to bridge the gap between jargon-heavy papers and the kind of wonder that made you stare at the stars as a kid. No prior knowledge required. Just curiosity.

"If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough." — attributed to Einstein (probably apocryphal, but still true)
